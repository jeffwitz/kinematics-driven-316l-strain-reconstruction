# Milestone 2 — the local-coefficient inverse, without the network

Registered before any run. Thresholds here are frozen; if the instrument cannot
reach one, that is a result to record, not a number to move.

## Why this milestone, and why now

Four measured results have lowered the numerical barrier far enough that it is
no longer the binding risk:

* the full-field matrix-free operator passes, adjoint at 4.4e-17 over 22.3
  million unknowns;
* thousands of local coefficients cost the mechanics **nothing** — 64 against
  4096 gave identical 8 Newton and ~170 Krylov;
* a real elastic-plastic front does not destroy the preconditioner, 14 -> 44
  GMRES per Newton and saturating;
* in the warm regime a coefficient perturbation falls back to **1 Newton**, 19
  Krylov.

So the question moves from *is this computable?* to **is the local plastic
representation identifiable and predictive?** That is the central scientific
risk and nothing measured so far addresses it.

The reduced integration domain is paused, deliberately. It is a second-order
optimisation that adds its own approximation — sampling, reconstruction,
certification — and complicates exactly the gradient this milestone must
qualify. Worse, it would be tuned against the J2/MFront bench, which may not
share the cost profile of the final model at all: if the generator produces an
admissible plastic field directly, part of the constitutive cost being
hyper-reduced simply disappears. The condition for resuming it is registered at
the bottom of this page.

## The chain under test

```text
a  ->  Delta p(x) = sum_j w_j(x) sum_k a_jk phi_jk(x)  ->  P_H  ->  equilibrium  ->  J(u; u_obs)
```

with the gradient by adjoint, and **no network**: the coefficients `a_jk` are
the optimisation variables directly. Mixing generator learning into an
unqualified inverse chain is how one gets a result nobody can attribute.

`w_j` is the bilinear partition of unity already used in the nonlinear bench,
`phi_jk` are `q` low-order polynomials in patch-local coordinates. At `q = 1`
the parameterisation reduces **exactly** to the field the bench already
assembles, which is the consistency check that the new code did not change the
old meaning.

`P_H` enforces thermodynamic admissibility. Two bounds, not one:

* `Delta p >= 0`, without which the dissipation is not sign-definite;
* `Delta p < Delta p_max(x)`, the driven-J2 admissible bound, which the material
  raises on rather than silently accepting. Optimisation steps *will* reach for
  it, so `P_H` clips at a registered safety fraction of it and the margin is
  reported at every iterate.

## Gates, in order, each blocking the next

**Gate 1 — the transpose is the transpose.** The dot-product identity for
`jacobian_transpose_action` against `jacobian_action`, over both the
displacement and the plastic slot, relative error **<= 1e-10**. Exact linear
algebra, so it earns a tight bound.

**Gate 2 — the gradient is the derivative of the objective.** Central finite
differences over a step sweep spanning at least four decades, on a small grid:

```text
| [J(a + h v) - J(a - h v)] / 2h  -  grad J . v |  /  |grad J . v|   <=   1e-5
```

at the best step, **and** the sweep must show the V-shape — truncation falling
as `h^2`, roundoff rising as `1/h`. The V matters more than the floor: a wrong
gradient produces a flat plateau at the size of the bug, and a threshold alone
would not catch a plateau that happens to sit low. Tested at `q = 1` and
`q = 3`, and on two random directions.

**Gate 3 — twin recovery, noiseless.** Generate `u_obs` from a known `a_true`
through this exact forward model, start from a flat `a_0`, and optimise.
Registered: objective down **>= 3 decades**, and recovered field error

```text
|| Delta p - Delta p_true ||_2 / || Delta p_true ||_2   <=   5 %      at q = 1.
```

Coefficient-space error and the `q = 3` case are **reported without a
threshold** — first observation, and the partition of unity may carry a
nullspace that makes field recovery the honest criterion rather than coefficient
recovery.

This gate is the point of the milestone. `A` is surjective and fitting the DIC
proves nothing about a field; what a twin tests is whether the *inverse in
coefficient space* is well posed when the data were generated by exactly this
model. If recovery fails here, it cannot succeed on real data, and no amount of
network capacity repairs it.

**Gate 4 — admissibility holds throughout.** Zero points violating `Delta p >= 0`
after `P_H`, zero solver admissibility raises, and `max(Delta p / Delta p_max)`
reported per iterate.

**Gate 5 — the warm regime survives optimisation.** Median Newton per
optimisation step **<= 2**, with at least **80 %** of steps at `<= 2`. The 1
Newton measured for a small perturbation is not guaranteed to survive real
descent steps, and if it does not, the cost model for the whole programme
changes.

**Gate 6 — the real DIC objective.** Only after 1-5. The elastic lifting must be
built with an explicit Kelvin/engineering/Voigt conversion and an asserted
interior residual, as the two live scripts do; the dozen legacy `scripts/*_p43.py`
retain 32 % of interior residual and no number from them may be reused here.
Registered: the DIC misfit descends monotonically over 50 steps and the
admissibility and warm-regime gates continue to hold. **No threshold on the
misfit itself** — a low misfit against a surjective operator is not evidence,
and setting a target would invite exactly the wrong conclusion.

## Registered falsifiers

* The finite-difference sweep shows a plateau rather than a V. The gradient is
  then wrong regardless of how small the plateau is.
* Twin recovery stalls with the objective still high, or reaches a low objective
  with a field far from `Delta p_true` — the second is the more dangerous
  outcome and means the representation is not identifiable from displacement.
* The warm regime degrades under real steps, so that optimisation costs cold
  solves.
* `P_H` is active on a large fraction of points at the optimum, which would mean
  the descent is being driven by the projection rather than by the data.

## What this milestone deliberately does not do

No network, no generator, no crystal plasticity, no reduced integration domain,
no full-field production run. `q` stays small. The point is a qualified inverse
chain, not a result about 316L.

## The registered condition for resuming the RID

Not a matter of taste, and fixed now so it cannot be argued after the fact.
Once a first local/network plastic chain runs in the warm regime, profile one
optimisation step:

| measured | decision |
|---|---|
| `T_constitutive < 30 %` of `T_total` | RID stays closed |
| `30 % <= T_constitutive < 40 %` | reconsider, with the profile in hand |
| `T_constitutive >= 40 %` | resume the RID specification immediately |

and independently: if moving to SRIX or crystal plasticity makes MFront dominate
the run again, resume regardless of the percentage above. The specification is
already written at `validation/constitutive_hyperreduction_preregistration.md`
and the rationale at `validation/reduced_integration_domain_rationale.md`; the
split is implemented and tested. Nothing is lost by waiting, and the measurement
turns the RID from a new project into a prepared answer to a measured problem.
