# What can be claimed, and what comes next

**Category: Explanation.** Is the current software reconstructive,
identificatory, or predictive?

## Reconstruction

The current code reconstructs mechanically admissible fields for an experiment
that has already been performed. DIC supplies boundary kinematics and the
heterogeneous local descriptors; equilibrium and the constitutive law supply
the interior mechanical field.

This is already more informative than differentiating DIC displacement, but it
is not forward prediction.

## Identification

The micromorphic extension seeks an effective interaction strength and spatial
length that explain the measured band morphology. The current F0/F1 evidence
supports distinct effects of $H_\chi$ and $\ell$. F2 confirmation and
unchanged-parameter transfer are still required.

Until then, the terms **spatial length** and **effective interaction** are used.
**Material internal length** is not claimed.

## Prediction

The local maps $\sigma_{y0}(x,y)$ and $K(x,y)$ are obtained from the observed
case. A model that requires these maps cannot predict the same experiment
before it occurs. Predictive use would require independent descriptors or a
microstructure-based constitutive model with transferable parameters.

## Path to crystal plasticity

The MFront boundary and the three-dimensional condensation adapter were built
so that a genuine 3D law can replace isotropic J2 behind the unchanged
two-dimensional plane-stress solver. A crystal-plasticity behaviour could use:

- measured microstructure and orientations;
- crystallographic slip mechanisms;
- independently identified material parameters;
- arbitrary transverse shear generated by orientation.

The local plane-stress adapter remains the efficient production path for the
present isotropic J2 model. The 3D condensed path is the extension point. Its
algorithmic contract is documented in
{doc}`../reference/numerics/three_dimensional_condensation`.

## Conclusion

> The present result is a verified mechanical reconstruction with a supported
> effective spatial interaction. Prediction and a transferable material
> length remain future claims, not current ones.
